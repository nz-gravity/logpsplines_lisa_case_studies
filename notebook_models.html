Simulation and likelihood notes#

The four notebooks estimate channel PSDs and the full XYZ spectral matrix. This page collects details that are useful after reading the walkthroughs.

What is generated#

MatrixSimulation uses a full LISA-shaped XYZ reference matrix constructed from orbit-dependent TDI-2 covariance kernels. It forms lower-triangular spectral factors at eight Keplerian orbit epochs. A relative eigenvalue floor of \(10^{-12}\) keeps numerical null modes positive; the same rule is used in generation, reference calculations and exact covariance controls.

All epochs act on shared stationary Gaussian Fourier noise. The real outputs are combined using piecewise-linear weights \(a_e(t)\) that sum to one. With a prescribed, smooth positive-definite residual matrix \(R_{\rm inj}(f)\),

\[x(t)=\sum_e a_e(t)\operatorname{irfft}\{F_e(f)R_{\rm inj}(f)^{1/2}\xi(f)\}(t).\]

The generator’s factor and evolutionary spectral matrix are

\[F(t,f)=\sum_e a_e(t)F_e(f),\qquad S_{\rm gen}(t,f)=F(t,f)R_{\rm inj}(f)F(t,f)^\dagger.\]

This is a controlled Gaussian spectral-matrix simulator, not a link-level instrument simulation or an implementation of noncommuting TDI delay operators. It deliberately separates known response structure from unknown matrix shape. The response controls dip positions; the fitted residual need not find them from scratch.

The stationary XYZ data freeze the geometry. Channel A is formed from a frozen-geometry XYZ draw and multiplied by a slow amplitude envelope; its local spectrum is the stationary channel spectrum times the envelope squared. The seven-day XYZ data keep the residual stationary while the reference evolves. The notebooks estimate neither individual noise allocations nor component levels.

Units and real-series conventions#

Time is in seconds in saved data; plots use elapsed days or hours. Frequencies are in Hz, plotted in mHz. Channel data are fractional frequency, and physical one-sided PSDs have units 1/Hz. The latent DC coefficient is zero. Nyquist uses a real draw and the real response, as required by a real FFT.

The time-varying notebook calls the public local_wishart_grid function with Hann windows. For its one-channel complex coefficients, 2*Y is the power sum and 2*counts is the number of real degrees of freedom under the local Whittle approximation. No extra unknown physical-unit conversion is fitted. Numerical scaling uses training powers only and is explicitly undone.

Finite windows change leakage and dependence. A local evolutionary spectrum need not equal an expected finite-window periodogram. Hann tapering reduces leakage; it does not establish frequency independence or posterior calibration.

Standard multivariate estimation#

The XYZ notebooks use a deliberately approximate sine reference. At each orbit epoch, the three known four-link loop times \(\tau_j\) give

\[g_j(f)=2i e^{-i\pi f\tau_j}\sin(\pi f\tau_j) =1-e^{-2\pi i f\tau_j},\]
\[S_{0,e}(f)=\operatorname{diag}(g_e)B_\star(f) \operatorname{diag}(g_e)^\dagger.\]

\(B_\star\) is a full first-generation Michelson covariance envelope frozen at the initial Keplerian geometry. Only the loop factors follow the known analysis geometry. The generator instead evolves the full response, so the reference is not an injected truth spectrum. No dip parameters or component noise levels are fitted. The sine factors place the dominant nulls, while the frozen envelope retains additional known frequency structure.

Let \(L_{0,e}=\operatorname{chol}(S_{0,e})\) and interpolate these factors with the same epoch weights. The fitted model is \(S_{\rm fit}(t,f)=L_0(t,f)R_{\rm fit}(f)L_0(t,f)^\dagger\). The ordinary multivariate library spline describes the full correction \(R_{\rm fit}\), not independent corrections for X, Y and Z.

Notebook 3 follows the inverse-Cholesky construction in Vajpeyi et al., arXiv:2607.04833:

\[R_{\rm fit}^{-1}=T^\dagger D^{-1}T,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(e^{h_1},e^{h_2},e^{h_3}),\qquad T_{jl}=-\theta_{jl}\ (j>l),\quad T_{jj}=1.\]

Three log-diagonal fields and six real/imaginary regression fields describe all auto and cross spectra. The pinned library integrates Gaussian theta weights analytically and restores their joint conditional draws before reconstruction. Both XYZ notebooks use two NUTS chains for the remaining log-diagonal weights and smoothing scales. Marginalisation is exact for the chosen Whittle target. It does not change that observation approximation.

The reported multivariate point is a mean of whole reconstructed matrices, which preserves positive definiteness. Elementwise matrix medians need not. Coherence shown in a notebook is computed from its displayed point matrix.

Notebook 3 calls local_wishart_grid(TimeSeries(...)) for eight Hann-tapered blocks spanning 0.4–99 mHz. The sine reference is propagated through the same nominal block window and the complex vectors are whitened before pooling. fit(WishartData, StationaryConfig(method="nuts")) runs the ordinary multivariate model without a custom likelihood. Counts retain all eight blocks. The nominal circulant block covariance approximates the original long-record finite-window covariance.

Both XYZ fits use the library’s stationary prior on the correction. This is a different prior on the physical spectrum from a raw-spectrum spline fit. The reference transformation preserves fixed-matrix likelihood differences before the declared window/coarse-graining approximations; it does not make these two prior models equivalent.

Short segments, reference correction and adaptive bins#

The seven-day approach takes inspiration from Burke et al., Noisy Pipelines I. It is not a reproduction of their complete instrument/conditioning pipeline.

Notebook 4 fits \(R(f)\) in \(S(t,f)=L_0(t,f)R(f)L_0(t,f)^\dagger\). The inferred correction is frequency-only; the reference supplies orbital time variation. A changing residual would require additional time fields.

The nominal Fourier reference propagates all epoch cross terms and the actual GapLike gate/taper. Its per-frequency complex matrices are checked against the full joint covariance. Native coefficients are whitened before pooling. The observation model still approximates the conditioned matrix as \(C_w(f)\simeq A_{w,0}(f)R(f)A_{w,0}(f)^\dagger\). Conditioning and a frequency-dependent correction need not commute.

The adaptive pilot uses observed gapped training data only. Both fits share the same partition, fixed before inference. Unsmoothed complex products and actual counts are summed; native bins in prescribed null neighbourhoods remain separate. “Adaptive” does not mean the likelihood changes during NUTS. The residual is approximately constant within an aggregate; this is not a posterior error guarantee.

The reference adapter uses the library’s unchanged stationary prior, max-normalized derivative penalties with a \(10^{-6}\) ridge and \(\sigma\sim\mathrm{HalfNormal}(1.28)\). The stationary eight-block fit uses the library’s observation-based initialisation. For the seven-day fit, protected singleton matrices have zero empirical conditional variances and give unusable starting values. A small process-local compatibility context starts the log-diagonal correction at zero and smoothing scales at 0.3; theta remains integrated, not fixed. The pinned StationaryConfig does not expose an initializer argument, so this context replaces only empirical_init_values during fit, then restores it. A numerical test verifies the unchanged log target; no library files or environments are modified. Both fits use the library’s information-scaled coordinates. The reconstruction adapter restores endpoint multiplicity when evaluating the library’s stationary breakpoint basis on the native grid. Tests compare this reconstruction with the library’s own fitting-grid evaluation.

Exact gapped point benchmark#

For a fixed subset of Fourier bins, collect both real and imaginary XYZ coefficients in a real vector \(y\). Propagating the same epoch, matrix and window operators yields its full covariance \(C(R)\) and exact Gaussian target

\[\log L(R)=-\tfrac12\{\log|C(R)|+y^T C(R)^{-1}y\}+\mathrm{constant}.\]

This includes channel, frequency, quadrature and epoch cross terms under the declared simulator. It is exact for the retained features, not the entire three-channel record. An independent dense time-domain operator test checks normalisation and all covariance blocks.

The fourth notebook evaluates the approximate sine reference, a reconstructed matrix and an injected-matrix oracle on independent realizations. Each candidate’s covariance propagates its own spectral factors through the complete observation operator; the oracle uses the original full-response generator. The oracle is used only for this final control. The benchmark does not optimize a component model, fit a joint posterior, or analytically marginalise theta under this different likelihood. Outside the fitted band, the recovered residual is extended constantly at the nearest boundary; the score includes that assumption.

The NUTS fit remains an independent-frequency Whittle approximation. Its window ENBW correction approximately tempers information; it does not restore the omitted covariance. Each inference notebook displays chain traces, divergences, rank-normalized split R-hat, bulk/tail ESS and tree-depth hits. All retained chains enter reconstruction. The displayed posterior intervals condition on the reference and Whittle model; they are not a demonstrated coverage result for the full time-domain data-generating process.
